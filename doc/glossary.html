NGS Genotyping Pipeline Documentation: Glossary

Here is an alphabetical list of terms used in the NGS Genotyping Pipeline. It is by no means exhaustive, but aims to provide a useful reference for new users.

A-D

  • Amplicon - a piece of DNA that is the source and/or product of amplification or replication events (e.g. PCR products)
  • Alignment - the process of arranging sequences to identify regions of similarity, aka matching
  • Allele - a variant form of a gene that occupies a specific position on a chromosome. An allele may take several forms and require multiple primers for detection, and many different target sequences for matching
  • Adapter clipping - the process of removing adapter sequences from the ends of DNA reads before analysis
  • Consumables - the various reagents, plates, and other materials used in the pipeline
  • Custom manifest - a user-created file that describes the layout and contents of a custom sample plate, either 96 or 384-well. See File Formats
  • DNA plate - a 384-well plate used for holding DNA from samples in the genotyping pipeline

E-H

  • Echo - an acoustic liquid handling robot used for precise dispensing of reagents in the genotyping pipeline
  • Echo plate - a specific (and expensive)384-well plate used in the Echo liquid handling system for holding samples or reagents. These plates have DNA, primers, taq polymerase, water, and DNA index barcodes added to them
  • Exact matching - a method of sequence comparison that requires perfect identity between the query and reference sequences. It is very fast
  • Experiment - a collection of all the data and information related to a particular genotyping run, including samples, consumables, amplicons, and results. It exists as a subfolder with the prefix "run_", and contains all the run files, critically the "experiment.json" file, which contains the complete record of every action performed as part of the run
  • Fastq - a file format used for storing DNA sequencing data, which includes both the DNA sequence and its corresponding quality scores
  • Genotype - the genetic makeup of an organism, typically represented by a collection of specific alleles (genetic variations) present at specific loci
  • Genotyping - the process of determining the genetic makeup of an organism by analyzing its DNA sequence
  • Global alignment - a method of sequence comparison that aligns the entire length of the query sequence with the reference sequence, allowing for gaps and mismatches

I-L

  • Index barcode - a short DNA sequence added to samples for identification during sequencing.
  • Indexing - the process of adding index barcodes to samples for identification during sequencing
  • Inexact matching - a method of sequence comparison that allows for some differences between the query and reference sequences. Slower than exact matching
  • Information panel/viewer - a section of the interface that displays specific information about the experiment, such as loaded samples or activity logs
  • Local alignment - a method of sequence comparison that aligns a portion of the query sequence with the reference sequence, allowing for gaps and mismatches
  • Log entry - a record of an event or action taken within the experiment, including details such as time, severity level, and message
  • Log level - a categorization of log entries based on their severity, such as info, warning, error, etc.

M-P

  • Manifest - an input file that may contain records for more than one plate of samples
  • Matching - the process of comparing sequenced DNA reads to reference sequences to determine genotypes, can be exact or inexact, local or global, also known as alignment
  • Miseq - a type of sequencing platform used in the pipeline for sequencing DNA samples
  • Nimbus - a liquid handling robot used for plate combining and sample preparation in the pipeline. Made by the Hamilton company
  • NGS - Next-Generation Sequencing (e.g. with the Illumina Miseq platform)
  • NGSG - NGS Genotyping (Pipeline), the physical process and software application for managing and executing the genotyping process using NGS technologies
  • Parsing - the process of analyzing and extracting information from a file or data structure
  • PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) - a technique used to amplify specific DNA sequences, used first to amplify target regions before sequencing, and then to attach index barcodes
  • Plate - a flat, rectangular container with wells used for holding and organizing samples or reagents in the pipeline
  • Primer - a short strand of DNA that serves as a starting point for DNA synthesis during PCR amplification
  • Primer plate - a 384-well plate used for holding primers in the genotyping pipeline
  • Processes - in the context of allele matching/sequence alignment, the number of independent computational tasks being executed

Q-T

  • Quality trimming - the process of removing low-quality bases from the ends of DNA reads before analysis
  • Read - a sequence of DNA that has been sequenced from a sample, which is analyzed to determine genotypes
  • Reference sequence - a known DNA sequence used as a benchmark for comparing and aligning sequenced reads
  • Rodentity - a database containing information about mouse ear punch samples, including their genotypes and other metadata
  • Run - a specific execution of the genotyping pipeline, encompassing all the steps from sample loading to result generation, also referred to as an experiment
  • Sample - a biological specimen or aliquot that is subjected to genotyping analysis
  • Sequence matching - the process of comparing sequenced DNA reads to reference sequences to determine genotypes, can be exact or inexact, local or global
  • Short read - a brief (~100-300 base pair) DNA sequence obtained from high-throughput sequencing technologies, e.g. Illumina Miseq
  • Stage - a distinct phase of the genotyping pipeline, each with specific tasks and objectives (e.g. Load, Nimbus, Primers, etc.)
  • Tab - a subsection within a stage that organizes related tasks and information (e.g. Load Samples, Load Consumables, etc.)
  • Taq polymerase - a DNA polymerase enzyme used in PCR amplification to synthesize new DNA strands, loaded in 6-well plates and dispensed into Echo plates
  • Threads - the number of concurrent processes or tasks that can be executed simultaneously in the pipeline. Similar to processes

U-Z

  • Water - provided in 6-well plates and dispensed into Echo plates. Required for PCR amplification at the Primer and Indexing stages
